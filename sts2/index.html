Part I

Fundamentals That Win Any Run

Before we open the tomb on any class, internalise these truths. They decide more runs than any single card pick. Miss these and even the best deck archetype collapses at the Act 3 boss.

The card-evaluation hierarchy

At every card reward, ask these questions in order. Stop at the first "no":

  1. Does it advance my scaling plan? Scaling is the engine — Strength, Poison, Focus, Stars, Doom, Osty buffs, Forge. Without scaling you lose to Act 3 bosses no matter how much block you stack.
  2. Does it solve a concrete current weakness? No reliable AOE? No turn-1 block? No card draw? If a card fixes a drafted weakness at the right cost, take it.
  3. Can I play it on the turn I want to play it? A 3-cost bomb you only play on turn 4 is often worse than a 1-cost you play every turn. Energy curve matters more than raw power.
  4. Will it still be good at the Act 3 boss? Early-game filler like single-target 6-damage attacks fall off hard. Scalers and multi-hits stay.

The Prior You Forget

At least one in three card rewards in a good run should be skipped. The most common Ascension-20 mistake is taking a "fine" card that dilutes draw. A thin deck draws its win condition turn 1; a bloated deck draws Strike, Defend, Strike.

The shape of a healthy deck

Floor Size

20–30 cards is the sweet spot for most archetypes. Below 15 you start burning Exhaust/Void. Above 35 your scalers are diluted.

Scaling Ratio

You need roughly 2–4 scaling cards (powers, synergy engines). More is gravy, fewer is death.

Block Floor

Every deck needs 3–5 reliable block sources for turns where you can't out-damage the incoming hit — bosses hit 30+ uncontested.

Energy Sources

At least one reliable card-draw engine and an energy relic by end of Act 2. Without them your 3-cost bombs rot in hand.

Act progression — the universal prior

Act 1 · Exposition

Play to survive, not to win. Take reliable attacks, one defensive tool, and pick the elite fight only if you can handle 20 incoming. The Act 1 boss rewards patience.

Act 2 · Commitment

Your scaling plan must come online here. Fight every elite you can survive — elite relics are the single largest power spike in the run. Start removing starter cards aggressively.

Act 3 · Execution

You already know your build. Route toward rest sites over shops, upgrade your two or three finishers, and save a single large potion for the boss opener.

Alternate Acts · Variants

STS2 early access ships three acts with alternate layouts and encounter variants rather than an Act 4. Runs currently end after the Act 3 boss — Mega Crit has signalled a True Ending is planned for a future EA patch, but the Heart fight from the original is not in the game yet.

Tip — Spend gold early

Shops in Act 1 are usually a waste; shops in Act 2 are where runs are saved. Prioritise card-removal services over any card or relic under 100g. Each remove fixes your deck and denies your opponent (the next bad draw).

STS1 vs STS2 — what changed

Every card and relic named in this guide has been cross-checked against STS2-specific sources (the STS2 wiki, Untapped.gg STS2 card database, Mobalytics STS2 hub). A few well-known STS1 cards are not in STS2 and are not mentioned here as picks: Reaper (removed), Corpse Explosion (removed for Silent), Grapple (deprecated). STS2 is in early access — the card pool will keep shifting, so always verify against the current patch before a run.

The Ironclad

The Ironclad

"The soldier who sold his soul — still paying for it."

The Ironclad is the safest introduction to the Spire and, simultaneously, the character with the most glass-cannon ceilings. His identity is built on three pillars — Strength as a damage multiplier, Exhaust as a deck-thinning and power-trigger engine, and self-damage as a scaling cost. He is the only class with a starter relic that heals after every combat, so mistakes forgive themselves when other classes would slowly bleed out.

His weakness is that his damage ceiling is real but linear — he never breaks into the absurd burst seen on late-game Defect or Regent. That means against hard elites and Act 3/4 bosses, you must genuinely have a plan: a multi-hit card that benefits per-hit from your Strength, an exhaust payoff that doesn't die to draw variance, or a Body Slam window that survives the setup.

Ironclad wins when you pick a lane early (by the second card reward of Act 1) and commit. He loses when you assemble three uncommon cards from three different archetypes and hope synergy will emerge. It won't.

Starter Relic — Burning Blood

Heals 6 HP at the end of every combat. This is the single most important new-player training wheel in the game. It converts small HP chip into free survival, letting you take greedy lines in Act 1 with minimal penalty.

Starting HP

80 HP — the highest in the game. Combined with Burning Blood, you have the largest HP buffer for experimentation. Use it to take one extra elite in Act 1 that another class would skip.

Signature Cards in Starting Deck

Five Strikes, four Defends, a Bash (deals damage and applies Vulnerable), and an Ascender's Bane curse on Ascension 10+. The Bash is a keeper; the rest are removal fodder.

What he cannot do

No innate poison, no orbs, no mid-combat summons, no multi-target free scaling. AOE is bought on cards like Whirlwind or Cleave — build it in or lose to hallway fights.

1 · Strength (Heavy Hitters) Consistency

The cleanest win condition in the game. Stack Strength, multiply hits, win fights.

Strength adds flat damage to every attack-card hit. On a single-hit card, that's linear; on a multi-hit card, it multiplies. Whirlwind with +6 Strength is not +6 damage — it's +6 per hit, applied to every target. The core loop: drop a Strength-gain card early, then pound multi-hit attacks for the rest of the fight.

This is the archetype you default to if rewards are ambiguous through Act 1. It works with any deck size, plays well under low energy, and survives card-draw variance because the power cards gain Strength once and keep paying forever.

2 · Exhaust (Cycling Engine) Ceiling

Delete your own cards for profit. The hardest-hitting Ironclad ceiling deck.

Exhaust plays the opposite game from Strength. Instead of scaling cards, you remove them — turning every exhaust into a power trigger, block generator, or free energy. The archetype needs three interlocking engines to come online: Dark Embrace (draws on exhaust), Feel No Pain (blocks on exhaust), and Corruption (skills exhaust and become free). Any two of these three are enough for a fully-operational infinite engine.

Corruption is the transformer card. Before it, Forgotten Ritual is a 1-cost skill; after it, Forgotten Ritual exhausts for 0 energy and triggers Dark Embrace. You draw every card, block every attack, and deal free damage via Pact's End or Ashen Strike. In a well-built Corruption engine, the only bottleneck is "how many turns can I chain this before the fight ends". The answer is often "all of them".

3 · Body Slam / Barricade Ceiling

Turn block into damage. With Barricade, block snowballs across every turn.

Body Slam deals damage equal to your current Block — a classic Slay the Spire win condition that returns unchanged in STS2. Without Barricade, this is a once-per-turn burst at your current block cap. With Barricade (block persists between turns), you accumulate block every round and dump it all on the boss's ouchy turn. The build's secret weapon is Juggernaut, a power that auto-damages the highest-HP enemy every time you gain block — it continues dealing damage on turns you don't cast Body Slam at all.

This is Ironclad's "I can't die" deck. It's not the fastest, and it struggles against enemies that drain block (certain Act 3 elites with block-removal), but for learning Ascension runs and for durable Act 3 boss attempts, it's the safest path. Take it when your early Act 1 offers tier-A block cards (Shrug It Off, Iron Wave, Battle Trance) instead of scalers.

4 · Perfected Strike (Hall of Strikes) Ceiling

Keep every Strike. Yes, really. Now turn them into a cannon.

A contrarian build that inverts the universal advice "remove Strikes". Perfected Strike deals a minimum of 6 damage plus a bonus per Strike-named card in your entire deck (deck + draw + discard). Pommel Strike adds card-draw, Twin Strike adds double-Strength instances, Headbutt cycles Strikes back, and (critically) Hellraiser adds more uniquely-named Strikes that all count toward the bonus. Two upgraded Perfected Strikes with eight Strike-type cards in deck deal upwards of 40 damage per cast.

The archetype is skill-intensive. Your mid-fight draw quality is noticeably worse than other Ironclad lines because you intentionally keep low-floor cards. It rewards runs where you pick up energy relics (Cursed Key, Red Skull) and card-draw options that keep your hand cycling. Without card draw, this deck is a single-finisher flash in the pan.

5 · Bloodletting (Rupture / Feed) Ceiling

HP is a resource. Spend it to go faster.

Bloodletting decks pay HP to gain Strength (Rupture), draw cards (Bloodletting the card), or nuke AOE (Inferno). The reason it works: Ironclad's starter relic heals every combat, so the HP you spend in fights is effectively free — so long as you survive. The reason it doesn't always work: self-damage from your own cards bypasses block, so it stacks with enemy chip to ambush you on variance-heavy turns.

You need a Rupture for the scaler to function and at least two self-damage cost cards. Feed is a run-defining pick — permanent max HP gain plus high damage. Combined with Rupture, each Feed kill permanently scales your max HP and current Strength in future fights. This archetype transitions seamlessly into Strength or Exhaust mid-run if Rupture doesn't show.

The Silent

The Silent

"A thousand cuts. Patience and poison."

The Silent is Slay the Spire 2's tactical rogue — the highest-ceiling, highest-skill class when piloted correctly, and the fastest way to lose if piloted poorly. She has no Burning Blood safety net, no free healing, no raw HP advantage. What she has is card draw, debuff density, discard-as-resource, and two of the most powerful scaling axes in the game: Poison and Shivs.

STS2 adds the Sly keyword — when a Sly card is discarded, it plays itself for free. That single mechanic transforms her old "discard is a downside" design into a "discard is a second form of draw". Acrobatics, Tactician, Calculated Gamble, and Reflex become setup tools that let you chain huge turns.

Her starter relic, Ring of the Snake, gives you two extra cards on turn 1 — which means turn 1 has seven cards to work with. That's enough to set up a poison power, drop a Cloak and Dagger for early block, and still attack. A strong turn-1 setup is the Silent's superpower; the best Silent players treat every fight as a chess opening.

Starter Relic — Ring of the Snake

Draw 2 additional cards on your first turn each combat. The best turn-1 relic in the game. It converts into two extra setup actions in every single combat — worth more than any other starting relic across a 30-combat run.

Starting HP

70 HP — lower than Ironclad, higher than Defect. No combat healing, so every HP loss is persistent. Plan routes around rest sites and campfires more aggressively.

Core Mechanics

Shivs — free 0-cost 4-damage attacks generated by skills. Poison — at the end of each enemy turn, they take Poison damage and lose 1 Poison. Discard synergies — cards that pay off when you discard from hand.

What she cannot do

No raw scalers equivalent to Strength (Poison scales, but only against single targets effectively). No self-heal. No easy block until Act 2. Elites with Artifact counter Poison entirely — have a Plan B.

1 · Shiv (Blade Dance) Consistency

Generate dozens of 0-cost 4-damage attacks. Multiply them with Accuracy and Shuriken.

The Shiv deck turns the Silent into a blender. Skills like Blade Dance, Cloak and Dagger, and Infinite Blades add free Shivs to your hand. Each Shiv by default deals 4 damage for 0 energy. The multiplier cards — Accuracy (+3 to every Shiv hit forever this combat), Infinite Blades (generates Shivs every turn), and After Image (gain 1 block per card played) — turn 4-damage chip into 10+ damage each, with five to ten per turn.

Shivs are Attack-typed, so they benefit from Dexterity (for block? no — from Strength and from attack-hit relics like Strike Dummy, Kunai, Shuriken). Every three Shivs played in a turn triggers Shuriken for +1 Strength. A deck with Accuracy + Infinite Blades + Shuriken cycles into unstoppable scaling. Your single biggest vulnerability is early damage before Accuracy lands — always take one defensive tool before committing.

2 · Poison (Venom Cycle) Ceiling

Slow start, infinite ceiling. Apply Poison, wait. Watch bosses melt.

Poison is the Silent's highest-ceiling archetype and, along with Defect's Focus build, the game's most durable scaling system. Poison damage ticks every enemy turn and doesn't care about enemy block, Weak, or Artifact-less buffs. The build needs three pieces: a Poison applicator (Noxious Fumes for passive, Deadly Poison for burst), a Poison multiplier (Catalyst doubles stacks), and a cycle engine (Prepared, Tactician, Reflex, or Calculated Gamble).

The new Sly keyword turbocharges Poison. Discard-driven builds that previously wasted good cards now play them for free on the same turn. A Tactician discarded from hand plays itself, generates energy, and keeps the engine humming. Combine Sly discards with Catalyst (doubles Poison stacks) for a turn-3 boss-delete combo that one-shots the Time Eater analogue.

Hard counter — Artifact

Any enemy with Artifact eats your first Poison application. Against Act 3 elites with stacked Artifact, you will lose unless you either (a) have enough stacks to burn through it or (b) have backup damage. Always maintain a single-target finisher like Finisher, Envenom + Shivs, or Grand Finale for the Artifact encounter.

3 · Sly (Discard Combo) Skill cap

The STS2 signature. Every discard is a free cast.

Sly is the Silent's brand-new mechanic and the reason to care about STS2's class redesign. A Sly card discarded from your hand plays itself at no cost and doesn't count as a card-play for restrictive effects. The moment you combine Tactician (Sly) with Calculated Gamble (discard hand, draw that many), your entire hand can chain in a single turn — every discarded Sly playing, every non-Sly reshuffling, and you doing it all with 7+ cards drawn on turn 1 thanks to Ring of the Snake.

The killer card is Master Planner — it makes all your non-Sly skills act as Sly. Suddenly Cloak and Dagger discarded plays itself for free, adding Shiv and block. Suddenly Noxious Fumes can be cast mid-turn for free. The deck ceiling is among the highest in any STS game — but the skill floor is brutal. Mis-sequence one discard and you lose your turn.

The Defect

The Defect

"Orbs, algorithms, overclocked vengeance."

The Defect is the methodical, engineering-mind class. His entire identity is orb management — small damage/block/resource satellites that orbit above him. Each orb has a passive effect (triggers end of turn) and an Evoke effect (triggers when consumed). Lightning orbs chip damage; Frost orbs generate block; Dark orbs cook for huge Evoke burst; Plasma orbs fuel extra energy; Glass orbs deal AOE. Every build is a question of slots, Focus (a stat that amplifies every orb), and what sequence of channels gives the best Evoke order.

STS2 buffs the Defect in two significant ways. First, he gains access to the Ironclad's old Status-card synergies — meaning he can use trash status cards as payoff triggers. Second, his starting orb slot count, Focus scaling, and Capacitor card all combine into the fastest-ramping orb engine in the game. His ceiling is borderline absurd: a mid-Act 3 Defect can Evoke 150-damage Dark orbs every turn while generating 60 block.

His weakness is setup time. Without a ramp draw, he dies to Act 1 elites that punish turn-1 block (the Sentry-likes, the Lagavulin-like that scales before you do). Prioritise survival in Act 1, Focus in Act 2, and Evoke payoffs in Act 3.

Starter Relic — Cracked Core

At the start of combat, Channel 1 Lightning orb. A constant baseline of damage and a free orb slot's worth of presence. Modest in absolute terms, but always active — never a wasted fight.

Starting HP

75 HP. Middle of the pack. Combined with slow ramp, take rest sites aggressively in Act 1.

Orb Types

Lightning — passive chip, Evoke burst. Frost — passive block, Evoke block. Dark — passive grows (6+Focus/turn), Evoke nukes. Plasma — passive energy, Evoke energy. Glass — passive AOE, Evoke AOE.

Focus

Every point of Focus increases every orb's passive and Evoke value by 1 (Plasma excluded). Stacking Focus is the single highest-leverage activity in any Defect run.

1 · Frost-Lightning Standard Consistency

The balanced default. Block and damage from the same engine.

This is the Defect equivalent of "just play the game". Channel a Frost on turn 1 for block, a Lightning on turn 2 for damage. Pick up Coolheaded for draw, Ball Lightning for damage + orb, and Glacier for bulk Frost. Every orb passive stacks with Focus. By Act 2 you're generating 15-20 block and 10 damage per turn without consciously trying.

The consistency of this deck is its strength. It doesn't need a specific rare to win — it scales linearly with good picks. A lone Defragment (permanent Focus +1) is enough to carry the deck through Act 3. Two Defragments and a Capacitor (more orb slots) wins on Ascension 20.

2 · Dark Orb Evoke Nuke Ceiling

Cook a single Dark orb for 20 turns. Evoke it. Kill anything.

Dark orbs gain damage equal to 6 + Focus every turn they sit in a slot. Evoking them deals all accumulated damage to the lowest-HP target. Combined with Capacitor (more slots to cook in), Defragment (higher per-turn growth), and Blizzard / Doom and Gloom (spike Evoke damage), a single Dark orb can accumulate to 100+ damage — then double or triple that on Evoke.

The key sequencing trick: you always channel Dark orbs after your Frost and Lightning orbs, so Dark sits on the right side of your slot row. When slots are full and you channel another orb, the leftmost orb Evokes — meaning your Frost orbs Evoke for block while your Dark orbs cook. Use Coolheaded to shuffle orbs; use Recursion to re-Evoke a single valuable orb. This deck wins long single-target Act 3 boss fights more reliably than any other Defect build because Dark's speciality is dumping a round's worth of accumulated damage on a single target in one Evoke.

3 · Claw Spam (Zero-Cost Loop) Explosivity

This is the Defect's "solo engine" build. It doesn't need Focus, orbs, or expensive rares. What it needs is card-draw quality. Every Act 2 card reward that isn't a Claw, a draw card, or a copier is a skip. Reboot effects destroy the deck — once your Claws are on the battlefield, shuffling them back is the whole plan.

The Regent

The Regent

"Stars are cheaper than iron. Spend them freely."

The Regent is one of two brand-new STS2 classes and arguably the most ambitious. Two resources run parallel: Energy (standard, reset each turn) and Stars (Regent-only, persistent across turns, uncapped). Stars fuel spell cards — X-cost attacks and skills that scale with how many Stars you pour in. This decouples "what you can play this turn" from "how much damage you can deal", a design that enables some of the highest burst turns in the game.

The Regent's second axis is Forge, a permanent in-combat enhancement to the Sovereign Blade — a signature attack card the Regent starts with. Every Forge-tagged card permanently adds damage to the Blade for the rest of the combat. A heavily forged Blade in Act 3 hits for 60+ damage on a single 1-cost card. Between Stars and Forge, the Regent has two distinct, viable win conditions — and some hybrid builds that use both.

The Regent's challenge is resource discipline. Stars are not reset each turn — they're precious. The second-worst decision pattern in STS2 is panicking on turn 2 and spending 8 Stars on Stardust for mediocre damage, then having no reserves for the boss mechanic. Regent pilots learn to hoard Stars like a miser through Act 1 and only spend when the kill math adds up.

Starter Relic — Divine Right / Crown of Stars

At the start of each combat, generate 3 Stars. This is your Stars baseline — every fight opens with 3 banked, before any card is played. Treasure every point: it is the most active starter relic in the game.

Starting HP

72 HP. Squishy. No combat healing. The Regent's fragility is offset by the Stars-as-bank design — you can reserve across fights to the extent that relics allow.

Signature starting card — Sovereign Blade

A 1-cost attack that deals a baseline damage and is permanently enhanced by Forge cards played during the combat. Never transform it. Never upgrade another card first.

What he cannot do

No Poison, no Orbs, no Strength, no Shivs. He can't chip — he can only burst. Early-game multi-enemy rooms are his hardest challenge; plan for AOE or run cards like Gamma Blast.

1 · Stars Burst (Stellar Engine) Ceiling

Bank Stars for three turns. Unleash a Stardust that kills the boss.

The Stars archetype plays like a caster in a typical RPG. Setup turns generate Stars via Gather Light, Glow, Hidden Cache, and Solar Strike. Payoff turns spend them through Stardust (a spell attack that multi-hits based on Stars) or Radiant (AOE nuke). Critically, Alignment converts Stars into Energy at a favourable ratio, meaning you can sometimes play your entire hand on a Stardust turn — and Stardust at 9 Stars hits for roughly 9 × 10 damage upgraded. That's a 90-damage single-target finisher on turn 3.

The deck is exceptionally well-equipped for Act 3, where burst trumps consistency. Its Act 1 weakness is that you need survival tools to reach the burst — Cloak of Stars, Reflect, Lunar Pastry (relic) are your insurance cards.

The Necrobinder

The Necrobinder

"Death is a delay, not an ending. Let Osty do the work."

The Necrobinder is STS2's second debut class and the most mechanically unusual unit in the game. Her identity is built around three pillars: a persistent minion (Osty), the Doom execute debuff, and previously-downside mechanics (Ethereal, Souls) reframed as advantages. She is the most "engine builder" of the classes — a proper Necrobinder deck looks like a tangle of interlocking parts, where the whole is greater than any single card.

Her starter relic, Osty's Binding, summons Osty at the start of every combat. Osty is a one-HP skeletal hand that attacks enemies, soaks hits, and can be grown via Summon-tagged cards. By Act 3, a well-fed Osty has 40+ HP and deals 15+ damage per turn while shielding you. He is the single most defining character feature in the game — and a playstyle unlike anything else in the Spire.

The Necrobinder rewards deeper reading of the board than other classes. Do enemies attack this turn or block? Does Osty get one-shot, or does he tank three hits? Is Doom stack X + turn N enough to execute before the enemy's big attack? She asks more of the player — and gives more in return.

Starter Relic — Osty's Binding

At the start of combat, summon Osty (1 HP, strikes enemies independently). Osty intercepts attacks meant for you — including lethal ones. He effectively doubles your HP bar.

Starting HP

68 HP — the lowest in the game. Necrobinder leans on Osty as her real health pool; her face HP is a timer.

Core Mechanics

Summon — grows Osty's HP and damage. Doom — stacks on enemies; when HP falls to that stack level, they die. Souls — zero-cost "exhaust after draw 2" cards. Blood Magic — spend HP for effects.

What she cannot do

No Strength, no Shivs, no orbs, no Stars. All her damage is either through Osty, through Doom execution, or through direct attacks. Early-game reliability is poor — many runs feel rough in Act 1.

1 · Osty (Minion Command) Consistency

Your minion attacks. Your minion blocks. You sit back and draw.

Osty decks build a skeleton tank. Early Summon cards (Snap, Sic 'Em, Pull Aggro) feed Osty HP and trigger attacks. Osty HP persists across turns — he's not a card-block that disappears. By Act 2, Osty soaks 25+ damage per turn (which would otherwise hit your tiny HP pool) and deals 10+ damage back. You play the role of a commander — summon, buff, trigger combos, and protect your minion.

The archetype's strength is turn-one value. Osty shows up automatically. Summon cards cost 1. By the second turn you've already set up a meaningful board. Its weakness is enemies that target you directly and bypass Osty — Act 3 elites with bypass debuffs force you into contingency plans.

2 · Doom (Execute Debuff) Ceiling

Doom is the Necrobinder's most AOE-friendly archetype. Scourge at 3 stacks across four enemies at the end of a hallway fight means every enemy dies at the end of their turn. The drawback: you must survive until Doom triggers. You need block. Block. More block.

3 · Ethereal + Souls Skill cap

Turn your downsides into the engine. The Necrobinder's cleanest infinite.

It's one of the higher-skill-floor decks in STS2 — but also one of the most satisfying when it works.

Boss & Elite Priors

General rules for surviving the moments that decide runs. These hold across classes.

Act 1 Boss

Hits hard in single large attacks. Prioritise one-turn block spikes (Shrug It Off upgraded, Gather Light, Pull Aggro). Stacker archetypes haven't scaled yet — use potions liberally. Keep one strong finisher upgraded.

Act 2 Boss

Scaling pressure: punishes long fights more than short ones. This is where Poison, Dark orb cooking, and Forge decks peak. Have at least one reliable AOE for multi-enemy boss variants.

Act 3 Boss

Status-card inducing and Artifact-equipped variants. You must have:

  • A scaler that doesn't rely on the first effect landing (for Artifact).
  • AOE or multi-hit for minion-spawners.
  • At least 15 block on turn 1 — setup turns die here.

Act 4 / True Ending (Not in EA)

The Heart fight and a fourth act are not in Slay the Spire 2 early access. Runs currently conclude with the Act 3 boss. Mega Crit has confirmed a True Ending is planned for a future EA patch — until then, the best completion goal is the Ascension ladder for each class.

Potion strategy

Potions are the most underused resource on Ascension 10+. Rules:

  • Save for bosses. Don't drink on a normal hallway fight if you're at 60%+ HP.
  • Drink on elites if you'd take 15+ damage otherwise. You will regret nothing.
  • Drink Strength potions before finishers. The extra +2 per hit on Whirlwind/Twin Strike is the difference between 20 and 60 damage.
  • Fairy in a Bottle is priceless. If a shop sells potion slots, buy them for it alone.
  • Alchemist event — transform two potions into a rarer one. Not always worth it. Common → uncommon yes; uncommon → rare usually no (too many bad rares).

Shop prior

A disciplined shop spend is the hidden skill of the Spire. Priors in order:

  1. Card removal service (always, unless your deck is already under 20 cards).
  2. Energy relic (if you don't have one by Act 2).
  3. Rare card that finishes your archetype (Limit Break, Accuracy, Noxious Fumes, Defragment, etc.).
  4. Relic that matches your build.
  5. Potion slots or the Calipers-tier relics.
  6. Nothing — it's fine to leave a shop without buying if nothing lines up.
